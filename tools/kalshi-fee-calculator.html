The Kalshi fee formula

For most markets Kalshi charges a fee on each trade, set by the contract price and the number of contracts:

fee = round up( 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P) )

where C = number of contracts
P = price per contract, in dollars

A 50¢ contract traded 100 times costs $1.75 in fees. A 10¢ or 90¢ contract costs $0.63 for the same 100 contracts.

A resting (maker) order that executes pays a lower rate, scaled by a multiplier M that Kalshi sets for each series:

fee = round up( M × 0.0175 × C × P × (1 − P) )

M defaults to 0, so a series pays no maker fee unless Kalshi’s table lists one. At 50¢ and 100 contracts, M = 1 costs $0.44 and M = 2 costs $0.88.

Source: Kalshi fee schedule and its PDF (formulas effective July 7, 2026). Last checked November 12, 2026.

The fee peaks at 50¢ and shrinks toward 1¢ and 99¢, because the formula multiplies price by (1 − price).

Check your confirmation

Kalshi rounds fees per fill and carries rounding across the fills of one order, so the fee on your trade confirmation can differ from this figure by a fraction of a cent. Some series carry different multipliers or extra fees. The number on your confirmation governs.
