Why fees change the answer

A contract pays $1 if it wins and $0 if it loses. Without fees, the expected value of buying at price P with true win chance p equals p − P. Kalshi charges a fee on each trade, so the true hurdle sits above the price.

EV per contract = p − P − fee per contract
break-even p = P + fee per contract
fee = round up( 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P) )

At 42¢ and 100 contracts the taker fee costs $1.71, or about 1.7¢ per contract. An estimate of 43¢ shows a 1-point gross edge and a negative result after the fee.

Fee-adjusted Kelly

The fee raises the cost of each contract from P to c = P + fee. With a $1 payout, full Kelly becomes:

f* = (p − c) ÷ (1 − c)

The calculation uses the unrounded fee per contract, so the stake does not depend on the order size. Compare it with the Kelly criterion calculator, which sizes without fees.

Your estimate drives everything

This tool measures edge against a probability you supply. It cannot tell you whether that estimate holds up. A small error in your estimate can outweigh the whole fee. Fees follow Kalshi’s schedule effective July 7, 2026. Maker orders pay 0.0175 × M, where Kalshi sets M for each series (source), last checked November 12, 2026. The fee on your confirmation governs.
